# IOU

What Homburg owes back to the work it stands on.

The idea of an IOU folder is User1's, and is set out in full in Obelus (https://github.com/silberpunkt/obelus, in its `__IOU`). In short: a pull request says, take my change, as I wrote it, now. An IOU says, here is what we learned by using your work; it is yours when it suits you. The folder is "a record of giving-back and a notebox of thank-you notes", and a debt that is settled or excused comes to rest in `__paid`, with a line saying how and why.

## Open

Nothing, on a technical level. Homburg is one file of its own program. It uses no one else's code, fonts, sounds or pictures, and so has taken nothing that it could hand back improved.

## Thank-you notes

These are debts of another kind. They cannot be paid, and are kept here so that they are not forgotten.

**To Charles Sanders Peirce (1839 to 1914).** For the existential graphs: a sheet, things set side by side on it, and a closed line that denies what it holds. Everything Homburg does is his three rules, drawn.

**To his archivists.** His papers at Houghton Library, Harvard, run to some hundred thousand pages, and were left in no order. They can be found, cited and read because people have spent working lives keeping, cataloguing and editing them. "With gratitude for their work, we have found delight." (User1)

**To the radio and plasma wave group at the University of Iowa, who keep space-audio.org.** They turn what spacecraft have measured, from Voyager to the Van Allen Probes, into sound and into spectrograms that anyone may hear and look at. Voyager's plasma wave instrument sampled the electric field on its antenna 28,800 times a second, four bits to a sample. They play those measurements back at the rate they were taken, smoothing the edges of the packets and changing nothing else, which they call "the moral equivalent of hooking our antennas up to an amplifier in order to drive speakers". It is a pickup, and they kept the tape.

The first Halic picture sent User1 back to their recordings, and Sphere Music followed from that. Homburg uses none of their recordings. What it owes them is the idea that a measurement may be listened to.
