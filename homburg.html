The Homburg

It is not known whether Charles Sanders Peirce wore a Homburg hat. He was an American logician, scientist, mathematician and philosopher, often called the father of pragmatism, and one of the circle in Cambridge, Massachusetts that he called the Metaphysical Club, with William James and Oliver Wendell Holmes Jr., later a Justice of the Supreme Court. He may well have worn one: the Homburg came into fashion in the 1880s, in the last thirty years of his life, as the top hat was beginning to give way.

Unlike a top hat, a Homburg is known for the crease along the middle of its crown, which our Homburg form, _[ ]_, has in its single space. We take his hat as an enclosure for his rules of logic on a sheet of paper. In his case that was often, it is said, the verso, the reverse, of his own old manuscripts. He left so many writings, some hundred thousand pages, that archivists have found difficulty. With gratitude for their work, we have found delight.

Peircean logic

This page carries out the rules of a Peirce sheet. Type a graph in the line above the paper and it is drawn: the paper with its margin, each cut as a circle, each scroll touching the wall of what holds it.

The graphs are the existential graphs of Charles Sanders Peirce (1839 to 1914). On his sheet, whatever is written is asserted; things side by side are asserted together; and a closed line drawn round something, a cut, denies what it holds. From those three he built all of propositional logic.

What was checked, on 17 November 2026: his name and dates, the Club and its members, the Homburg's coming in during the 1880s, and the size of his papers. What was not confirmed: that he wrote on the versos of old manuscripts, which is often said. What is said here of the graphs themselves, and of his shading them, is from memory.

⌨︎

The typed forms

The way of typing a sheet is PROWL's, settled by User1 with eniamb on 16 November 2026. The hat _[ ]_ is the sheet, and gives this page its name. Round brackets are cuts. An underbar before a cut makes it a scroll, read "unless".

The whole of the main pane is the paper, and takes the window's shape. The column at the left shows each sheet as text, in a line or descended. The switch above the column changes the column only. The paper does not care how it was typed.

◎

The picture

Three rules, User1's. The border is whole: no cut reaches the paper's edge. The sheet has a margin. And the underbar means contact: a scroll touches the inner wall of its host, which for a cut is its line and for the sheet is its margin. The small dot marks the place where the pen did not lift.

The paper is toned, between white and the grey of a filled form. Areas inside an odd number of cuts are filled, as Peirce came to shade them.

○◐●

Three views

View Choices sets how the sheet is drawn, and changes nothing in it. Textbook has perimeters, the margin, the verdict line and the dots. Bare has tones and names only. Halic, User1's, begins from Bare: when a case is tried the paper drops to half grey and each form is lit at its perimeter, white if it comes out true and black if false, fading to grey inward and outward, and never past the cell that holds it. A true name is white and a false one black. It is meant as a fudge and not as a proper fill.

≋

The march

Beside the views, March sets the Halic light moving. Metal sends ripples slowly through the greys, toward the rim of a true form and away from the rim of a false one, like a shine passing over metal.

Inversing turns the light slowly over, white to black and back, names and all. Half way round you are looking at the same sheet read the other way. Before the existential graphs Peirce made the entitative graphs: the same drawings, with a cut still denying, but with things side by side meaning "or" where here they mean "and". The half-way picture is that reading of the sheet, in the case where every name's truth is turned over: each form that was white is black, and a sheet that stood falls. It is the paper seen from its verso. Twice in each turn everything passes through the middle grey, and the form comes up again out of it. The turning-over is checked by the tests for every sheet and case; the account of the entitative graphs is from memory.

♫︎

Sphere Music

A pickup is carried round the ambit, a flat ellipse laid along the forms inside the margin, once in eight seconds. The grey it is over is what sounds: white sings high, black sings low, and grey is silence, louder the further from grey. So a true form is heard as a rise toward its rim and a false one as a fall, and crossing a wall is a step. The same lap, shown as greys, is the strip beneath the paper, a variable-density sound track in the round; it is the score, and what is at its gate is what is heard. The light is reckoned afresh here as a signed sum, which is the honest form of what Halic paints. It makes no sound until Play is pressed.

⚖︎

Trying a case

A sheet put on the paper, from the column or by Formalize, opens in the first case in which it stands, if it has one. No case sets it plain again.

Under the line each name can be set true or false, and pressing a name on the paper itself does the same. A name set false is struck through. A form that comes out true is bleached, and one that comes out false is tinted and drawn broken. The page leans the same way, inside its margin: lighter when the sheet stands, darker when it falls. Solve steps through the cases in which it does, and is absent when there are none. Broken means false, not wrong: a false cut inside another is often what makes the outer one true.

❝

In words

Above the line the sheet is said in words. This is worked out by rule from the sheet itself, so it follows whatever is typed. A cut round one name is "not"; round several, "not both". A cut holding a scroll is "if ... then". A scroll on the sheet is "unless". The words are plain and literal, and will sometimes be clumsier than a person's.

The box can also be written in. Write, say, if p then q; not r and press Formalize, or Enter, and the words are set as a sheet. Commas, "and" and semicolons all join; round brackets group. A name spelt like one of the grammar's own words is said, and written, in single quotes: if rain then 'or'. Words you have formalized are left as you wrote them for as long as the sheet is the one they made.

∅

What it asks

Nothing. It sends nothing and counts nothing. It keeps these things, in this browser only, so that it opens as it was left: the view and whether it marches, the last sheet, and the trues and falses given to it.

Rules of the Peirce sheet

1. Both ends of the frame are required: _[ opens and ]_ closes.

2. Spaces are never imputed. A space is required between two names, and before every _( . Everywhere else a space is air.

3. Any run of spaces counts as one.

4. Order on a level is air. The sheet has no left or right.

5. A name does not end in an underbar.

↴

Descended

_[]_        the head, on a line of its own
()  r       "not:"     an egg, leading its line
__  q       "unless:"  a stick, leading its line

A line is either peers, or a headword followed by what it holds. The headword also holds the lines indented beneath it. One complicated thing per line.

⊙

Rules of the picture

1. Whole border. No cut reaches the paper's edge.

2. The sheet has a margin.

3. The underbar means contact. A scroll in a cut touches the cut's line. A scroll on the sheet rests against the margin.

On this paper the last scroll in a cell touches at the right and the first, if there are two, at the left. A third in one cell is drawn free, with a hollow dot, since on one line there is no third place to touch.

The parts

Homburg is one file, homburg.html, with no libraries and nothing fetched. These are notes from eniamb to whoever next opens it to change it. Each part named below begins, in the file, with a comment in capitals. Search for the words given.

The program is in two halves. The first needs no page: it reads a sheet, weighs it, says it, lays it out and reckons its light, and can be run by itself. The second, after THE PAGE, draws and listens. The tests live beside the file and use only the first half: node homburg.test.js homburg.html

✂︎

Reading a sheet

Search: THE READER. tokens() cuts the line into marks and names, readInline() builds the sheet, and readDescended() reads the descended form. A name is { k: 'n', v } and a cut is { k: 'c', scroll, kids }, and each remembers its place in the line. Anything wrong is thrown as a Misspelt, with a message and a place, and the page shows both.

The trick: a space is demanded before every _( , so that p_(q) can never be mistaken for a name p_ beside a cut.

⊨

Truth

value() and holds() give a sheet's truth in a case. census() counts the cases in which it stands. solutions() lists them, the most affirmed first, and is what Solve steps through. shapeKey() writes a sheet with order taken out, which is how the page knows that two sheets are one.

⊕

The picture

Search: THE PICTURE, then THE SHEET. picture(sheet, measure, room) returns circles, names, a paper and a margin. Each cell lays what it holds along one diameter. The last scroll goes at the right end and the cell's circle is drawn through its far point, so the two touch there and nowhere else. Given the room of the pane, the paper is the pane.

The trick: nothing is solved for. Tangency is made by construction, and then checked by measurement in the tests.

✎︎

Drawing, and the three views

Search: THE TONES, THE THREE VIEWS. drawPaper() draws Textbook and Bare, and drawHalic() draws Halic. The tones are a handful of named greys at the head of that part; change them there. In Halic the whites of a cell are laid before the blacks, so that the order of typing does not show, and each form's outward light is clipped to the cell that holds it.

≋

The march

Search: THE MARCH. One SVG filter holds a table of 64 greys, and marchTable() rewrites it every frame. Metal adds a travelling wave. Inversing swings every grey about the middle by a cosine. The speeds are MARCH_SECONDS and TURN_SECONDS.

⇄

Words, and Formalize

Search: THE SHEET IN WORDS, FORMALIZE. sense() turns a sheet into a small formula, say() speaks the formula, and formalize() reads words back into a sheet. The tests keep them honest by sending every sheet out into words and back, and comparing truth in every case. That check has already caught a comma that read as "and" where "or" was meant.

The trick: do not trust the wording. Test the meaning.

♪

Sphere Music

Search: SPHERE MUSIC, THE SCORE, THE VOICE. lightAt() reckons the signed light at a point. lapTable() samples it round the ambit. scoreOf() turns the lap into two curves, pitch and loudness. lay() puts those curves on a fresh oscillator each time the sheet or the case changes, and fades the old one out. One gate before the loudspeaker does Stop.

The trick, learned the hard way: never cancel a curve that is running. Browsers disagree about what that means, and one of them left the sound on. Make a new voice and let the old one go. The pickup on the paper is drawn from the audio clock, so eye and ear cannot drift apart.

⌂

What is kept

Search: KEPT. One entry in the browser's local storage, homburg.last, holds the line, the case, the view and the march. Nothing else is stored, and nothing is sent.

☞︎

Changing things

To add a sheet to the column, add a line to EXAMPLES: a caption and an inline form. The page reads every example as it opens, and will stop with an error if one is misspelt, so look at the page after adding one.

Pitch is BASE and RANGE. Loudness is LOUD. A lap takes SLOW seconds. The reach of a form's light is REACH. The limit on how large the forms grow is GROW. The size of the text is the one font line in the body's style.

✓

The tests

homburg.test.js runs in node, with no browser. It tries every case of each sheet for its truth, sends sheets through both typed forms and through words and back, tries the misspellings, measures the picture for touching and clearance, and checks the light and the score. If you change a rule, change its test first and watch it fail.

What the tests cannot do is see or hear. Look at the page and listen to it, in more than one browser. It was tested on User1's side in LibreWolf, by eye and by ear. On eniamb's side it was seen and heard in Chromium 152, and run in Firefox 159 and WebKit 27.2, where no sound device was to be had and the sound was checked in each engine's offline renderer instead.
